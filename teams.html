Nobody chases anyone

This is the part that sinks most pledge drives. The event goes well, and then someone spends three weeks with a spreadsheet, working out who owes what, sending reminders and making phone calls nobody wants to make.

None of that lands on you. The moment the scores are in, every sponsor is invoiced automatically for their own pledge against their own student's score. Stripe sends the invoice, sends the reminders, and takes the payment.

You watch it from your dashboard — who pledged, who has been invoiced, who has paid. After thirty days the books close. Whatever came in came in, and nobody spends the season asking a neighbour for twelve dollars.

Nothing to buy, nothing to hand out

Most fundraisers need a prize ladder — trinkets at $35, a water bottle at $50, a T-shirt at $100. Somebody has to choose it, buy it, store it and give it out, and the program is spending money before it has raised any.

Ours has the prize built in. There is a score, it goes on a board next to their classmates' scores, and a student wants theirs to be higher. That is the whole motivation, and it costs nobody anything.

It is fairer, too. A prize ladder rewards whoever has the wealthiest relatives. A score rewards whoever plays best — which is what you wanted to reward anyway.

We charge the school nothing. Our fee is a flat 10% of each pledge, added on top of the sponsor's invoice as its own line — so the program is never billed, and never has anything deducted.

There is nothing else. No setup fee, no monthly fee, no minimum, no contract and no charge for the challenges themselves. The 10% is the only money we make.

And it is only earned on money that actually arrives. If a sponsor never pays their invoice, the program receives nothing for that pledge and neither do we.
