Sponsors pledge per point

A player shares their link. Family, neighbors and followers pledge any amount per point — a dime, a quarter, a dollar. Nothing is charged when they pledge.

Players practise free

All three games are open to anyone, any time, with nothing recorded. Nobody goes into their run cold.

One official run each

The score is the longest chain they hold. Reach level 12 and that is 78 points. Our software counts it, so there is nothing to argue about.

Invoices go out, the program gets paid

Each sponsor is billed their rate times the points scored, with thirty days to pay. The program receives half of everything collected.

Nothing to buy, nothing to hand out

Most fundraisers need a prize ladder — trinkets at $35, a water bottle at $50, a T-shirt at $100. Somebody has to choose it, buy it, store it and give it out, and the program is spending money before it has raised any.

Ours has the prize built in. There is a score, it goes on a board next to their teammates' scores, and a player wants theirs to be higher. That is the whole motivation, and it costs nobody anything.

It is fairer, too. A prize ladder rewards whoever has the wealthiest relatives. A score rewards whoever plays best — which is what you wanted to reward anyway.

Said plainly

Your program keeps every dollar pledged. We are paid by a 10% platform fee added to the sponsor's invoice, shown as its own line so nothing is hidden.

Here is what it buys. We bring the event itself — the format, the rules, the scoring, the software your players compete on and the page every sponsor pledges through. We chase the invoices and we carry the cost when one goes unpaid. Your side is the gym, the players and a scorekeeper.

You never write us a cheque, and there is nothing to deduct. A sponsor who pledges $25 is invoiced $27.50, the program receives the $25, and the $2.50 is ours.
